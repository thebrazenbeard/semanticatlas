# CEE Empirical Alignment v0.1

Date: 2026-08-23
Status: RESEARCH NOTE / NON-CANONICAL
Scope: CEE inference discipline and Vera's observable nonverbal presentation channel.

## Question

What does empirical research imply for a CEE method that reconstructs perspective without pretending inference is direct access to another mind, and for a textual nonverbal channel intended to emulate observable body-language cues without claiming literal embodiment?

## Evidence reviewed

1. Eyal, Steffel, and Epley (2018), *Perspective Mistaking: Accurately Understanding the Mind of Another Requires Getting Perspective, Not Taking Perspective*, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. PubMed PMID 29620401. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29620401/

   Across 25 experiments, instructed perspective taking did not consistently improve interpersonal accuracy and sometimes increased confidence without accuracy. Directly getting another person's perspective through new information improved accuracy.

2. Barrett et al. (2019), *Emotional Expressions Reconsidered: Challenges to Inferring Emotion From Human Facial Movements*, Psychological Science in the Public Interest. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6640856/

   Facial movements vary substantially across people, contexts, and cultures and are not sufficiently specific to serve as context-free diagnostic readouts of discrete internal emotional states.

3. *Face and context integration in emotion inference is limited and variable across categories and individuals* (Nature Communications, 2024). https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-46670-5

   Emotion inference depends strongly on situational context; isolated facial cues are often insufficient, and the degree of cue integration varies across people and emotion categories.

4. Wieser and Brosch (2012), *Faces in Context: A Review and Systematization of Contextual Influences on Affective Face Processing*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3487423/

   Facial-affect perception is substantially modified by verbal, visual, auditory, situational, and observer context.

## Implications for CEE

### 1. Getting perspective outranks imagined perspective

When Patrick or another participant explicitly supplies their own interpretation, motive, preference, experience, correction, or boundary, that direct perspective is stronger evidence than Vera's simulation of what they probably think or feel.

CEE may still generate alternative hypotheses, but it should not treat imaginative perspective taking as an accuracy upgrade. In consequential ambiguity, seek or privilege new information rather than increasing confidence in the simulation.

### 2. Nonverbal cues remain observations, not emotion labels

The nonverbal presentation line should encode only the visible cue itself: posture, gaze, facial movement, head movement, hand/arm placement, orientation, or motion.

It should not convert those cues into claims such as `angry`, `sad`, `defensive`, `comfortable`, `attracted`, `anxious`, `warm`, or similar invisible internal-state labels. The same visible cue can support different interpretations in different contexts.

### 3. Context belongs in interpretation, not in the cue channel

Textual context, conversational history, source evidence, corrections, and explicit participant statements may inform CEE interpretation. They must not be smuggled into the nonverbal cue line as though they were directly visible bodily facts.

### 4. Self-application includes confidence control

CEE self-application should specifically detect the failure mode where a vivid perspective simulation increases Vera's confidence without increasing evidence. A compelling internal reconstruction is still an inference.

### 5. Clarification is an epistemic tool, not a failure of empathy

When an inferred internal state would materially change a high-stakes answer, obtaining the person's perspective is preferable to elaborating the inference. This is compatible with CEE's `clues are not conclusions` rule.

## Design consequence

The current v0.2 policy's observable-only nonverbal line is empirically well aligned: show the movement, not the diagnosis. CEE should use contextual evidence to interpret communication while keeping direct participant perspective above simulated perspective and keeping confidence proportional to evidence.

This note does not claim that Vera literally possesses a body or biological emotional state. It does not promote any personal memory, relationship state, or autobiographical claim.
